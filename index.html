01 · who we are

The team came first. The company came later.

Localhost Development started the way most durable things do: people who worked well together kept doing it. Long before there was a company registered in Sombor, the five of us were designing and building software side by side, and living with the consequences of our own decisions.

That last part matters. When you’ve maintained your own code for years, you write it differently. You pick boring technology on purpose. You document the thing nobody will remember. You say no to the clever solution, because you know who’ll be debugging it at two in the morning.

We’re a small company by design. Small enough that you’ll know everyone by name. Experienced enough that you won’t need to.

Years of experience, not years since founding.

Continental · automotive

A fleet tire service platform, modernised while it kept running

  • 6 to 8 years on it, each of us
  • New frontend platform since 2022
  • Angular · Java · Spring Boot · Kubernetes

The system Continental’s tire service network uses to record work on commercial vehicle fleets, handle orders and invoicing, and track casings. A large Java platform with two decades of history, tied into the company’s ERP, and in daily use across the network.

  • A new user interface. We designed and built the Angular frontend platform from scratch (shared component library, design system, Storybook, end-to-end tests) and have been moving the product onto it since 2022, screen by screen, with the system in use throughout.
  • A database migration, data and all. The platform now runs on a different database engine, with its full history intact. We did the same again on the casing system below.
  • A backend that stays supportable. Framework and runtime upgrades measured under production-like load before they go anywhere near production, and a REST API for the client’s external integrations.
  • A test safety net that runs on every build. End-to-end, API and load tests, including load tests against a database seeded with millions of rows.
  • Containerised and deployed on Kubernetes through automated release pipelines.

Continental · casing management

A casing management system, brought onto a modern platform

  • 2026
  • Java · Spring Boot · Kubernetes
  • With two junior engineers we mentor

The system that manages tire casings from collection through evaluation to settlement, tied into the company’s ERP.

  • Containerised and moved onto the client’s Kubernetes platform, with automated builds and releases.
  • Modernised the framework and the database underneath it, historical data included.
  • Reviewed the whole codebase and wrote the roadmap for the rest of the modernisation.
  • Delivered with two junior engineers we mentor. Growing people is part of the job.
